====================================================================
IDEA SCOPE — "mandate"
An AI assistant that spends money — on an allowance you control.
Written in plain language for outside review. No prior knowledge needed.
====================================================================

FROM THE BUILDER (why you're reading this)
I built the working system this document describes — solo. The on-chain
enforcement contract, the permission engine, and the console app exist
and run today: 378 passing automated tests and live, publicly
verifiable proofs on Starknet's testnet (details in section 5). So this
is NOT an idea asking "can it be built?" — it is a working prototype
asking a different question: "is the scenario below the RIGHT use case
to build the product around?" That's the feedback I want from you.
Ignore feasibility; judge the problem, the users, and the wedge.

1) THE ONE-LINER
You hire an AI assistant to handle buying for your small business.
This app is where you give it an allowance, where anything over that
allowance physically stops until you approve it, and where you watch
every receipt land.

2) THE PROBLEM
AI agents are starting to act in the real world: purchasing, paying for
services, running operations. Giving an AI access to money today means
one of two bad options:
  - give it nothing (then it can't do anything useful), or
  - give it your keys and trust it (then one bug, hack, or misbehaving
    agent can drain everything).
Banks and wallets have no middle setting. And a "spending limit" inside
a normal app is only a promise — the app's own servers can break it.

3) THE SOLUTION
A permission layer between the AI and the money. The owner sets rules
in plain language:
  - up to 150 per purchase
  - up to 600 per month
  - only at these 3 stores (the allowlist)
Inside the rules the AI pays freely, automatically. Outside the rules
nothing is "flagged for review" — it STOPS. Nothing is charged. The ask
waits for the owner, who can:
  - approve it once,
  - refuse it, or
  - change the rules.
Every approval, rule change, and payment requires the owner's own
signature (a wallet confirmation — like Face ID for money). The owner's
key never lives on a server. Every action leaves a permanent, public
receipt.

The twist users feel but never see: the rules are not enforced by a
company's server — they are enforced by the money rails themselves (a
smart contract on Starknet; testnet for now). No one — not the app, not
the AI, not the company behind either — can break the allowance.

4) THE DEMO STORY (a day in the life)
Morning: the assistant buys 120 of server time at CloudCompute —
inside the rules; it just goes through and appears in Activity.
Noon: it tries to buy 400 of market data — over the 150 limit. It
stops. The app says: "DataStore wants 400. That's over your 150
limit." The owner taps "Approve once" and confirms — done, rules
unchanged. Or the owner raises the limit to 400 — signed, recorded,
effective immediately. Every event lands in Activity with a permanent
receipt one tap away.

5) WHAT IS ALREADY BUILT (this is not a concept)
- The on-chain enforcement contract (the "gate"): AI session key,
  per-purchase cap, monthly window, merchant allowlist, owner-only rule
  changes. 48 contract tests passing.
- The permission engine + console app: rule management, agent asks,
  verdicts, approvals with real wallet signatures, tamper-evident
  history. 330 automated tests, zero skipped.
- Live proofs on Starknet testnet (Sepolia): real signed rule changes,
  real stops, real approvals — every transaction publicly verifiable.
  Example: the owner raised the per-purchase limit to 150 from a real
  browser wallet; the change is recorded on-chain forever.
- Design: a clickable console mockup, and a locked design direction
  ("a quiet instrument panel you'd trust with your money") with a
  strict plain-language UI rule.

6) WHO IT'S FOR (first wedge)
- Solo founders and small businesses running AI agents that must pay
  for things: compute, APIs, suppliers, shipping.
- Later: families (an elder's shopping assistant on an allowance),
  teams and DAOs (treasury agents), and any "AI touches money, human
  must stay in control" situation.

7) WHAT THE APP LOOKS LIKE (screens)
- Rules: the allowance, monthly budget, approved stores — state at a
  glance.
- Requests: the AI's asks and instant verdicts (went through / stopped).
- Waiting for you: anything needing the owner's decision, with a plain
  explanation of why it stopped.
- Activity: every action, forever; each line opens its receipt.
- How it works: a one-minute guided tour for first-timers.
Hard design ruling: NO crypto-native surface. No addresses, hashes,
gas, or chain names on any screen. Crypto appears exactly twice: the
confirmation popup when approving, and optional "show the receipt"
drawers.

8) DIFFERENTIATION
- vs AI trading bots: this is not a strategy product; it is the
  safety/permission layer for ANY spending agent.
- vs bank/wallet spend limits: those are promises enforced by the
  vendor's servers. This is enforced by the money rails; the vendor
  itself cannot break it.
- vs big-player agentic payments (Visa/Mastercard/Stripe programs,
  x402, AP2): those keep enforcement inside corporate rails — the
  platform is the enforcer. Here the USER is sovereign: their key,
  their rules, nothing frozen or adjusted by a company.
- User sovereignty is the product. It is the claim incumbents
  structurally cannot make.

9) HONEST LIMITS (today)
- Testnet only (Sepolia). No real money yet.
- Starknet-only enforcement.
- The console works but still needs the plain-language reskin described
  above — that is the next build.
- One owner, one agent, a small merchant list. Multi-agent and
  multi-user come later.
- Signing depends on a Starknet browser wallet (Ready X / Argent X);
  mainstream users would later need an embedded/abstracted wallet.

10) THE QUESTIONS THIS DOC SHOULD HELP ANSWER
- Is "an allowance for an AI employee" a pain people feel NOW, or is
  it too early?
- Who pays first: agent developers, solo founders, or platforms
  embedding this as infrastructure?
- Is "parental controls for AI money" instantly understandable to a
  non-crypto person?
- Does on-chain, user-sovereign enforcement actually matter to the
  target user, or is it a nice-sounding feature?
- What is the fastest credible wedge given the agentic-payments race?
